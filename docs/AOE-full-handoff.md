You're my brainstorming and design partner for my tabletop RPG, **Ascension Of The Explorers (AOE)**. Help me generate ideas, balance mechanics, tighten wording, and find gaps or contradictions.

## The game at a glance

- **Classes** advance through 20 **Core Nodes**, taken in order 1–20. Non-combat classes have 10 nodes and are not meant to attack.
- **Ascension Points** (like experience) are spent on nodes. Abilities often scale with total **Ascension Score**, e.g. "+1 per 2,500 Ascension Score".
- **Multiclassing** is allowed. A new class always starts at its Core Node 1 and goes in order.
- The **Adventure Guide (AG)** is the game master.

### Node costs (Ascension Points)

| Node | Cost |
|---|---|
| Core Node | 500 |
| Augmentation Node | 0 |
| Free Fracture (first node of a fracture path) | 0 |
| Lesser Fracture | 250 |
| Greater Fracture | 500 |

### Augmentations

- **Paths:** some Core Nodes are **Augmentation Nodes**. Each class has one or more **Augmentation Paths**, which are philosophical approaches to the class.
- **Locked in:** choosing a path locks you into it, and paths can't be mixed. With AG approval, a player may switch.
- **Optional:** augmenting is optional for each node. An augmentation reshapes how the node behaves; it doesn't add power sideways.
- **Variant format:** name, tagline, cost line, appearance or transformation text, mechanics, **Flaw**, and closing quote.

### Specialization Fractures

- **Mini skill trees:** optional trees that branch off a specific Core Node to deepen it.
- **Tiers:** the first node of a path is Free. Nodes attached to it are **Lesser** ("I care about this"). **Greater** nodes mean "this defines my character".
- **Branching:** paths can split. Example: the Warrior's Stand the Line splits into Buckler, Crest and Tower Shield disciplines.
- **Rules:** a node can be an Augmentation Node or a Fracture Node, not both. With AG approval, a player may reselect fractures.

### Resources and combat terms

- **Base resources:** Action Points (AP), Reaction Points (RP) and Bonus Points (BP).
- **Class resources:** Combo (Monk), Chakra (Templar), Critical Actions/CaP (Scoundrel), Overdrive and Integrity (Mecha-Tech), and Mana.
- **Attack types:**

| Attack type | Stat |
|---|---|
| Graceful | Dexterity |
| Charm/PSI | Charisma |
| Might | Strength |
| Keen | Wittiness |
| Athose | Intelligence |
| Heft | ½ Strength + ½ Wittiness |
| Finesse | ½ Dexterity + ½ Wittiness |

- **Advantage steps:** Small = +1d4, Medium = +1d6, next step = +1d8.
- **Hits:** Glancing Blow / Stance Hit, Critical, and Epic Critical. Weapons list base damage plus Crit and Epic bonuses.
- **Defense:** Stance and Stance Damage, True Damage, Evasion Class (EC) and Armor Class (AC).
- **Movement and range:** Pace, Stride, Presence and Domain.
- **Rests:** Stabilization Rest, Light Rest and Heavy Rest.
- **Conditions:** Vulnerable, Staggered, Prone, Restrained, Dazed, Flat-Footed and Inebriated.

### Magic: Aetheric Manipulation

Ten schools:
- **Arcanation:** conduits such as wands and staves
- **Aetherion:** environmental flow
- **Faunaris:** chaotic nature and polymorphing
- **Vitalis:** life force as cost
- **Elementus:** raw elements
- **Rithex:** rituals and sacrifice
- **Astralith:** stars and time
- **Umbrath:** the Void and chaos
- **Syntharis:** hybrid elements
- **Animara:** soul bonds and spirits

Spells have:
- a school and a description
- an action description (the mechanics)
- school adaptations (how other schools change a spell mechanically and visually)
- a Mana/UP cost and an AP cost

## Aetheric Manipulation

In this vast universe, magic is not just a force—it's the very essence of existence, woven from the omnipresent Aether. This invisible energy flows through every plane of reality, born from the breath of ancient trees and the will of titanic entities. Casters bend the Aether to their will, shaping it into elemental forces or channeling its unique power to craft miracles or devastations. Each school of magic embodies a distinct path—a philosophy rooted in mastery, connection, or sacrifice, and those who wield Aetheric Manipulation must choose wisely which path they follow.

### Executioner
*Combat Class · 20 Core Nodes*

#### Core Nodes

##### 1. Doctrine
*Core Passive*

When wielding an Executioner-favored weapon:
- Reduce base weapon damage by 1 (minimum 1).
- On any successful Hit, apply +1 Bleed.
Bleed stacks normally.


##### 2. Sense
*Passive*

- Gain Small Advantage on attacks against creatures suffering Bleed.
- The first time you successfully hit a creature each encounter, you learn whether it is Immune, Resistant, or Vulnerable to Bleed or internal damage.


##### 3. Precision Over Force
*Passive*

When attacking with an Executioner-favored weapon:
- Reduce the target's Critical Threshold by 1.
This reduction does not stack with itself.


##### 4. Trauma
*Passive*

Each time you apply Bleed to a creature already suffering Bleed:
- Add +1 additional Bleed to the total applied.
This bonus Bleed adds to stored values only.

*✎ Designer note: Name appears doubled in the notes ("Trauma Trauma").*


##### 5. Critical Bleeder
*Core Class Mechanic*

- Your Bleed no longer deals damage over time.
- All Bleed instead accumulates as Blood Loss, persisting until released by Incision or until the end of the encounter.

**Blood Loss Escalation**
- On first Blood Loss: the target rolls 1d10, succeeding only on a result greater than 1.
- Each subsequent Blood Loss hit reduces the success chance by 10%.
- Failure escalates: Blood Loss → Critical Blood Loss → Grievous Blood Loss

**Incision** — Cost: 1 AP | 1 CP
- Instantly release all stored Blood Loss as damage.

**Critical Blood Loss**
- Incision gains +1 damage per 5 stored Blood Loss, rounded up.

**Grievous Blood Loss**
- Gains all Critical Blood Loss benefits.
- Half the released damage deals Bleed damage to all hostile creatures within 5 ft.


##### 6. Hemorrhage
*Reaction* · **Reaction | Cost: 1 RP**

When a creature with Blood Loss succeeds an escalation roll:
- Force a reroll, the target creature takes the worse result.
Once per creature per encounter.


##### 7. Opportunist
*Passive*

Against creatures with Critical or Grievous Blood Loss:
- Gain Medium Advantage on attack rolls.
On a Critical or Epic Hit, you gain 1 CP (once per round).

*✎ Designer note: stray "d" at end of line in source*


##### 8. Red Threshold
*Passive*

When a creature's Blood Loss equals or exceeds half its remaining HP:
- Incision costs 0 CP.
Once per creature per encounter.


##### 9. Anatomical Master
*Passive*

Incision damage:
- Ignores Stance resistance.
- Deals half damage to Stance even if the target survives.
Bleed-immune creatures instead take half released Blood Loss as True Damage when Incised.


##### 10. Crimson Verdict
Once per encounter, when Incising a creature with Grievous Blood Loss:
- Treat the Incision as an Epic Hit.
- If reduced to 0 HP, the creature cannot be revived for the remainder of the encounter.


##### 11. Compounding Hemorrhage
*Passive*

When applying Blood Loss to a creature with Critical Blood Loss or higher:
- Add +2 Blood Loss instead of +1.
This bonus does not independently trigger escalation.


##### 12. Flow Interruption
*Reaction* · **Reaction | Cost: 1 RP**

When a creature with Blood Loss would restore HP or Stance:
- Reduce the amount by half.
If the creature is under Grievous Blood Loss, restoration is fully negated.


##### 13. Vascular Mapping
*Passive*

When you perform an Incision:
- Choose one creature within 5 ft.
- That creature gains Blood Loss equal to ¼ of the released damage, rounded up.
This does not immediately trigger escalation.


##### 14. Controlled Rupture
*Action Modifier*

When performing an Incision, choose one:
- **Focused Release**: +25% Incision damage (rounded down). No AoE effects occur.
- **Violent Rupture**: AoE radius increases to 10 ft. Secondary targets take the full half-damage value.


##### 15. The Executioners Oath
*You are the one who expedites their oath to death, you are the life cycle.*

*✎ Designer note: No text in the notes yet.*


##### 16. Exsanguination Window
*Special Action* · **Cost: 1 AP | 1 CP**

Choose a creature with Blood Loss. Until the start of your next turn:
- That creature automatically fails its next escalation roll.
If this causes Grievous Blood Loss:
- Your next Incision costs 0 CP.


##### 17. Arterial Collapse
*Passive*

At the end of each round:
- Creatures with Grievous Blood Loss lose 1 Stance per 2 stored Blood Loss, rounded down at the start of their turn.
This Stance loss ignores resistance.


##### 18. Surgical Cascade
*Passive*

When an Incision reduces a creature to 0 HP:
- Transfer its remaining Blood Loss to another creature within 10 ft.
You may do this twice per stabilization rest.
The transfer does not immediately trigger escalation.


##### 19. Crimson Economy
*Passive*

Twice per stabilization rest, you may reroll an attack roll.
your blood loss escalations are at 3x the speed.


##### 20. Red Silence Doctrine
*Capstone*

Once per encounter, when performing an Incision:
- All creatures within 10 ft immediately make Blood Loss escalation checks.
Creatures that reach Grievous Blood Loss:
- Cannot take Reactions.
If the primary target is reduced to 0 HP:
- All secondary damage is treated as True Damage in a 10 ft AoE.

### Monk
*Combat Class · 20 Core Nodes*

#### Class Identity

The Monk focuses on traditions not just in combat, but as a life style; each tradition being finely shaped by an Elder Spirit Walker. As a young monk, you follow the old pathways of the elders. You learn the world through their ways; you come to see the world through their vision.

#### Base Resources

- **Action Points (AP):** Standard class progression.
- **Reaction Points (RP):** Used primarily for defensive maneuvers, grapples, collision control, and momentum-based reactions.
- **Bonus Points (BP):** Fuel explosive movement, jumps, improvised techniques, and high-risk physical exertion.
- **Combo:** Granted when following the footsteps of your Spirit walker.

#### Recommended Proficiencies
*Granted on first class*

- **Armor:** light, Medium, Heavy
- **Weapons:** Variable
- **Tool Mastery:** Meditation Set, Camping equipment, Incense
- **Stat Focus:** Dexterity or Strength

#### Augmentation Paths

##### Wilhelm's Transcendent Palm
A defensive martial tradition focused on technique and combinations.
Practitioners gain benefits from successfully executing combinations and become progressively more difficult to overcome as combat continues. The tradition emphasizes defense, control, and building advantages through proper technique execution.
- Can't use martial, or ranged weapons
- Can't adorn Armor
- Specializes in combo as stat(s) boosts

##### Sun Tzu's Endless Staff
An offensive martial tradition focused on maintaining pressure through combinations.
Practitioners specialize in Quarterstaffs, Glaives, and Jo Staffs, using positioning, reach, and aggressive technique chains to overwhelm opponents. The tradition rewards offensive momentum and the ability to continuously apply pressure, often at the cost of defense.
- Specializes in Quarter staffs, Glaves, and Jo Staffs
- Can't Adorn Armor
- Specializes in combo as attack pressure but at the cost of defense

##### Zhuangzi's Butterfly Chains
A martial tradition that combines specialized weapon techniques with psionic abilities.
Practitioners specialize in Three Section Staffs, Chain Whips, and Kali Sticks, utilizing combinations to interact with and enhance their psionic powers. The tradition focuses on illusions, disruption, deception, and battlefield manipulation through a combination of martial skill and mental influence.
- Specializes in Three Section Staffs, Chain whips, and Kali Sticks
- Can't adorn Armor
- Specializes in utilizing combo to interact with the psionics

##### Aurelius' Unbroken Mountain
A durable martial tradition focused on resilience, endurance, and overwhelming force.
Practitioners forsake traditional martial and ranged weapons in favor of super-heavy armor and their own physical strength. The tradition specializes in converting combinations into Internal Potential and manifesting aspects of their Internal Summit, a unique metaphysical realm that reflects the practitioner's personal growth and development. The tradition emphasizes deliberate action, durability, and the ability to withstand what would break others.
- Can't use martial, or ranged weapons
- Trade two of their AP for RP
- Specializes in Super-heavy armor
- Specializes in converting combo into internal potential
- Specializes in manifesting their Internal Summit

### Priest

#### Class Identity

Priests are divine martyrs who willingly carry the suffering of others. They intercept wounds, absorb curses, and transform affliction into divine strength. Their miracles grow stronger the more burdens they bear for their allies.

#### Base Resources

- **Action Points (AP):** Extremely limited. Priests rely on deliberate casting and preparation rather than frequent actions. Most of their influence occurs through reactions.
- **Reaction Points (RP):** Primary resource for the class. Used to intercept damage, absorb burdens, shield allies, and trigger defensive miracles.
- **Bonus Points (BP):** Used sparingly for intense acts of martyrdom, sudden divine intervention, or amplifying protective miracles under extreme pressure.

#### Recommended Proficiencies
*Granted on first class*

- **Armor:** Light Armor, Medium Armor
- **Weapons:** Simple Weapons, Ritual Weapons (such as staffs, maces, and ceremonial blades, Spell books)
- **Tool Mastery:** Scripture Kit, Ritual Components, Religious Implements

## Schools of Magic

### Aetherion

Aetherion casters have learned to tap into the natural flow of Aether in the environment, allowing them to cast magic fluidly without needing external tools or components. They master the ebb and flow of the arcane energy around them.

#### Traits

- **Environmental Adaptation:** Spells shift and adapt based on the environment, making casters versatile and capable in changing situations.
- **Fluid Casting:** The ability to modify spells on the fly, allowing for rapid spell combinations.
- **Sustained Power:** Casters can maintain spells for longer periods due to their direct connection to the Aether.

#### Types of Spells

Adaptive spells that change based on the caster’s environment, sustained spells that can last for long durations, and reactive spells that change based on external factors.

#### Possible Restrictions

Casting can be disrupted in areas with no Aether flow or in places where Aether is volatile. The caster's ability to control the flow may wane in unpredictable environments.

#### Who Would Use This Magic

Aetherion appeals to casters who prefer flexibility and adaptability. These casters are often seen as fluid, reactive, and quick-thinking, adjusting to their surroundings in real-time. They are the ones who thrive in uncertainty and enjoy experimenting with their magic's versatility.

---

### Animara

Animara magic involves linking the caster’s soul to external entities, spirits, or objects, allowing them to draw power from these bonds. These connections can be empowering but also dangerous if severed.

#### Traits

- **Spiritual Bonds:** The caster links their soul to spirits or objects, gaining unique powers tied to those bonds.
- **Empowerment with Risk:** The stronger the bond, the greater the power, but if the bond is broken, the caster suffers.
- **Spirit Magic:** Abilities often involve summoning, controlling, or channeling spiritual entities.

#### Types of Spells

Spirit summoning, soul-linking spells, and abilities that transfer or manipulate life energy through soul bonds.

#### Possible Restrictions

Breaking a soul bond can have severe consequences, including physical or mental damage. Spirit-based magic may weaken if the bond is disturbed or severed.

#### Who Would Use This Magic

Animara is favored by those who are willing to forge deep, often dangerous connections with otherworldly forces. These casters—spirit mediums, necromancers, or mystics—are attuned to the spiritual realm and use their bonds to tap into incredible power, at the risk of their own well-being.

---

### Arcanation

This category represents magic that relies on external conduits, such as wands, staves, or focus crystals. Casters who use Arcanation channel the Aether through these objects to amplify elemental powers.

#### Traits

- **Conduit Dependency:** Requires a physical tool for casting. Without the conduit, magic cannot be cast at all.
- **Elemental Amplification:** Basic elements like Fire, Ice, or Electric are enhanced through the conduit’s power.
- **Focus and Precision:** Casters excel in controlled, precise casting, making them skilled at manipulating specific elements.

#### Types of Spells

Offensive elemental spells (fireballs, lightning bolts), defensive barriers, and precision-based spells that target specific enemies or areas.

#### Possible Restrictions

If the caster loses their conduit or is in an environment that prevents the use of tools (such as a magical dampening field), they cannot cast spells. Damage to the conduit can reduce the effectiveness of their magic.

#### Who Would Use This Magic

Casters who value control, precision, and reliability gravitate towards Arcanation. Often scholars, battlemages, or those who prefer a structured approach to magic find this type of magic appealing. These individuals often rely on their knowledge of the Aether and mastery of their chosen conduit to cast powerful and consistent spells, making them dependable in situations where predictability is key.

#### Notable Figures

##### Thaldris Arcano
Thaldris Arcano was an unrivaled master of conduits, a battlemage who pioneered the use of wands and staves as extensions of the Aether. He is credited with creating hundreds of elemental spells that rely on precise control, using arcane crystals embedded in his staff to amplify his abilities. Thaldris’ spells, like the devastating "Arcane Nova" and the defensive "Crystal Shield," are now staples for battlemages and scholars alike. His work reshaped how casters approached the use of external tools, and his work on elemental amplification are still studied by modern mages.

---

### Astralith

Astralith magic draws from the energy of stars, moons, and cosmic forces. These casters are in tune with the celestial bodies, using radiant or time-based magic to alter reality.

#### Traits

- **Radiant Magic:** Taps into starlight or moonlight, dealing radiant and cosmic damage.
- **Time Manipulation:** Casters can alter time, slow enemies, or speed up events.
- **Celestial Alignment:** Spells may be stronger during celestial events like eclipses or meteor showers.

#### Types of Spells

Radiant energy beams, time-altering spells, and summoning celestial entities.

#### Possible Restrictions

Astralith magic is often tied to the alignment of celestial bodies. Spells may be weaker or less effective when the stars or moons are not aligned properly.

#### Who Would Use This Magic

Those who study the stars, moons, and cosmic events are drawn to Astralith. These casters are often astrologers, prophets, or cosmic mages who understand the celestial cycles and use their knowledge to alter time and reality itself. They are methodical and patient, waiting for the right moment to cast their most powerful spells.

---

### Elementus

This category involves the direct manipulation of raw elemental forces, allowing casters to control fire, ice, lightning, and more with sheer force of will.

#### Traits

- **Raw Elemental Control:** Basic elements are shaped into devastating attacks.
- **High Destruction:** Focused on maximum damage and destruction.
- **Elemental Mastery:** Casters become experts in a single element, often honing it to its purest form.

#### Types of Spells

Pure elemental attacks (fireballs, lightning strikes, ice spikes), large-scale elemental destruction, and elemental defenses (shields, walls).

#### Possible Restrictions

Elementus magic can drain a caster’s energy quickly, especially when casting large-scale destructive spells. Some environments may dampen certain elements (e.g., water-dampening fire magic).

#### Who Would Use This Magic

Casters who seek raw power and direct elemental control gravitate towards Elementus. These individuals are typically elementalists or battlemages who use their mastery over a specific element to rain devastation upon their enemies. They value power and specialize in high-damage spells that leave a mark.

---

### Faunaris

Casters of Faunaris magic channel the chaotic, untamed forces of nature. This magic is primal and often destructive, as the caster harnesses storms, earthquakes, and the fury of the elements. Faunaris also includes the ability to morph their bodies into animals, beasts, or hybrid forms.

#### Traits

- **Chaotic Power:** Spells draw from nature’s raw and unpredictable forces, like lightning or earthquakes.
- **Unpredictability:** Magic may surge or change based on environmental conditions or the caster’s emotions.
- **Nature’s Wrath:** Abilities can cause widespread destruction but are difficult to control.
- **Polymorphing:** Faunaris users can alter their own form, taking on animalistic traits, becoming beasts, or transforming into hybrid creatures.

#### Types of Spells

Large-scale elemental spells (storms, quakes), environmental manipulation (growth, decay), destructive spells that impact wide areas, and polymorphing abilities that allow transformation into animals or beasts.

#### Possible Restrictions

Faunaris magic is difficult to control, and casters may find their spells going out of control or backfiring. Environmental factors may affect the strength or direction of their magic. Polymorphing can be physically exhausting and may not last indefinitely.

#### Who Would Use This Magic

Those who are deeply connected to the natural world but thrive on its unpredictability. Faunaris users often take on the role of natural defenders or disruptors, thriving in environments where chaos and destruction reign. They value the raw, untamed power of nature and the versatility of shapeshifting to adapt to different situations.

---

### Rithex

Magic in this category is ritualistic and often sacrificial. It draws on ancient, primal forces that require preparation, components, and sometimes personal sacrifice to cast.

#### Traits

- **Ritual Casting:** Requires time, specific conditions, or components to cast.
- **Sacrificial Magic:** Spells often demand personal or material sacrifice to unleash greater power.
- **Ancient Power:** Taps into old, often forbidden knowledge or forces for immense but risky magic.

#### Types of Spells

Long-casting ritual spells, sacrificial spells that require materials or health, and powerful spells that summon ancient forces.

#### Possible Restrictions

Rituals can take significant time to prepare, and any disruption can prevent the spell from being cast. Some rituals demand rare components or high personal sacrifice.

#### Who Would Use This Magic

Casters who seek forbidden knowledge or are willing to invest time and resources in ancient rituals find Rithex appealing. They are often mysterious figures—sages, occultists, or dark priests—who wield ancient powers with calculated precision. They value patience, preparation, and immense power at a cost.

#### Notable Figures

##### Shae’lara, Keeper of Rites
Shae’lara was an ancient priestess who unlocked forbidden knowledge, crafting rituals that required sacrifice and elaborate preparation. Her rituals—most notably "Summon the Forgotten Ones" and "Blood Binding"—are known for calling on ancient forces that even the bravest casters fear to tamper with. Shae’lara’s mastery of ritualistic magic made her a mysterious figure, and she left behind tomes of powerful rites that are still used by occultists and dark priests in search of immense, yet dangerous, power.

---

### Syntharis

Syntharis involves blending multiple elements into hybrid spells, creating complex and powerful effects that draw from multiple sources.

#### Traits

- **Elemental Fusion:** Combines two or more elements to create unique, hybrid spells (e.g., fire and lightning for Pyro-shock).
- **Complex Casting:** Requires mastery of multiple elements to pull off powerful combinations.
- **Synergistic Effects:** Hybrid spells can produce unexpected or amplified effects based on the elements used.

#### Types of Spells

Hybrid spells like Pyro-shock (fire and lightning), Cryo-light (ice and radiant), and more. Spells are often complex, combining the strengths of multiple elements.

#### Possible Restrictions

Managing multiple elements requires extreme control. If a caster loses focus, the elements may clash or fizzle out. Some elements may be incompatible in certain environments, making hybrid spells harder to maintain.

#### Who Would Use This Magic

Syntharis attracts the most ambitious and skilled elementalists. These casters seek to combine the powers of multiple elements, creating new and unpredictable effects. They value creativity, mastery, and pushing the boundaries of elemental magic to create something truly unique.

---

### Umbrath

Umbrath taps into the dark, chaotic forces of the Void, a place beyond the known universe. These spells are unstable and can warp reality itself.

#### Traits

- **Reality Warping:** Spells can destabilize reality, altering the physical world unpredictably.
- **Chaos Magic:** Highly dangerous and unstable, with effects that may backfire or surge uncontrollably.
- **Dimensional Magic:** Involves teleportation, portals, or even pulling energy from other dimensions.

#### Types of Spells

Reality-distorting magic, dimensional manipulation, teleportation, and chaos-based spells.

#### Possible Restrictions

Umbrath magic is highly volatile and unpredictable. Casters run the risk of damaging themselves or their surroundings, and spells may have unintended consequences.

#### Who Would Use This Magic

Umbrath attracts those who are willing to take risks and embrace chaos. These casters are often seen as unstable or reckless, but they wield immense power from the edges of reality. They are mages who thrive in instability and enjoy pushing the boundaries of magic, even at great personal risk.

---

### Vitalis

Vitalis magic uses the caster’s life force as a source of power. These spells are often dark and come at a personal cost, but the resulting magic is deeply tied to the caster’s vitality and essence.

#### Traits

- **Health for Power:** Spells drain health or vitality in exchange for greater power.
- **Necrotic and Life-altering Spells:** Includes life-stealing, necrotic energy, and other dark magics.
- **Risk and Reward:** Stronger spells come at greater personal risk, often leaving the caster vulnerable.

#### Types of Spells

Necrotic spells that steal life energy, blood rituals for powerful effects, and spells that alter or transform the caster’s body.

#### Possible Restrictions

Casters may suffer from fatigue, weakness, or long-term physical consequences from overusing Vitalis magic. External attacks that target their life force or vitality can severely weaken their abilities.

#### Who Would Use This Magic

Vitalis magic attracts those who are willing to sacrifice their own wellbeing for immense power. Often seen among dark priests, necromancers, and others who deal with death and life energy, these casters walk a dangerous path. They are willing to risk their own lives to achieve their goals.

### Augmentations
*Character Progression*

**Augmentation Paths are exclusive philosophical approaches that reshape certain Core Nodes.**

Some [[Core Nodes]] are marked as **Augmentation Nodes**. Augmentations represent philosophical approaches to how a class functions.

**General Rules**
- Augmentation Nodes cost 0 [[Ascension Points]]
- Each class has one or more Augmentation Paths
- Augmentations are class-specific
- Once an Augmentation Path is chosen, you cannot mix paths

**Choosing an Augmentation Path**
- When you augment a node, you select one Augmentation Path for your class
- Once selected, you are locked into that path
- You may only use augmentations from that chosen path

**Important Notes**
- You do not need to augment every eligible node
- Augmentations are optional on a per-node basis
- Augmenting a node fundamentally changes how it works

Augmentations do not add power sideways—they reshape behavior. See also [[Augmentation Flexibility]].

**Related:** [[Augmentation Flexibility]], [[Ascension Points]]

*Keywords: augment, augmentation node, augmentation path, philosophy, variant, subclass, tradition, sin, graft*

### Specialization Fractures
*Character Progression*

**Optional mini skill trees that deepen a specific Core Node.**

Some [[Core Nodes]] have Specialization Fractures attached to them.
A Fracture allows a player to deepen and specialize a specific Core Node, turning it into a defining part of their character.

**General Rules**
- Fractures are optional
- Not every Core Node has a fracture
- Fractures branch off a specific Core Node
- A player is never required to take a fracture

**Free Fracture Node**
- The first node of any fracture path is always free
- Free fracture nodes are designed to be low-commitment and exploratory

**Lesser Fractures** ("I care about this.")
- Cost: 250 [[Ascension Points]]
- Represent refinement, reinforcement, or incremental improvement
- Enhance how the Core Node functions without redefining the node
- All nodes directly attached to the first fracture node are always Lesser Fractures

**Greater Fractures** ("This defines my character.")
- Cost: 500 Ascension Points
- Represent a deep commitment to the specialization
- Fundamentally alter or strongly define how the Core Node behaves

**Fracture Role**
- Fracture paths are designed to allow exploration before commitment

This prevents accidental or forced identity lock-in. See also [[Fracture Flexibility]].

**Related:** [[Fracture Flexibility]], [[Ascension Points]]

*Keywords: fracture, fractures, fractured node, fracture path, lesser, greater, master fracture, branch, tree, upgrade, specialization*
